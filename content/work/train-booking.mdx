## In a nutshell

Small improvements, large impact.

After shipping a new Train booking form in early 2023, **62.5% of users dropped off on page one**. We combined Amplitude analysis with live usability tests, then ran an ideation session with product, tech, commercial, and BI. An Impact × Implementation matrix narrowed us to three issues.

## Context

tiket.com’s Train product sits inside a 52M+ user travel platform. Train travellers compare prices across OTAs and are wary of accidental bookings. The new form had to feel transparent before it could feel fast.

## The three problems

<Callout eyebrow="Price breakdown">
People want the final price before payment. We only showed it on booking form page 2 — which users treated as a barrier.
</Callout>

<Callout eyebrow="Countdown">
The countdown was read as a payment timer, not a booking-session timer. That created fear of accidental purchase and more drop-off.
</Callout>

<Callout eyebrow="Passenger details">
The passenger form felt like a gate in front of the price. If that entry point isn’t obvious, people stall or leave.
</Callout>

## Solutions

<Steps>
  <Step number="01" title="Header: guiding instead of pressuring">
    We removed the booking countdown that caused early drop-off and added a stepper so people could see the path to payment.
  </Step>
  <Step number="02" title="Passenger details: copy and icon">
    Usability tests compared wording and icons. “Fill” invites action; a pencil marks information that can still be edited.
  </Step>
  <Step number="03" title="Price details: breakdown early">
    A price-breakdown entry point shows ticket fare and service fees before continuing — so travellers can compare OTAs without guessing.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Impact

The new flow won the A/B experiment: **+2.12% CVR** from booking form page 1 to purchase, and **90% of users** filled in passenger details.

## Reflection

I got to lead a workshop, work tightly with BI, and watch an experiment win. The same discipline — analyse, prioritise, change the smallest high-leverage surface — shows up across these tiket.com stories.
