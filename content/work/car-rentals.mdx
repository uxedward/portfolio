## In a nutshell

Revamping the business model and the experience.

Car Rentals was drawing complaints and “how do I even book this?” inquiries. We interviewed customers from support tickets, read Amplitude, ran usability tests, and used How Might We framing. The work touched education on the landing page and the confusing **Zone / Area** pricing model.

## Context

Car Rentals on tiket.com covers Indonesia, with or without a driver. The core flow is landing → search and vendor → product detail → booking form (pickup, drop-off, extra areas).

From October 2021 to February 2022, complaints clustered in two places:

- **No incentive to explore** — 44% general information, 20% how to order
- **Unclear business model** — 12% extra payment and zone issues

## Problem 1: no reason to explore

Users messaged customer service instead of browsing: how to book, what the rules were.

**62% dropped off the landing page** without reaching search. Only **8.5%** viewed rental regulations. We hypothesized a missing step-by-step guide and weak entry points.

<Callout eyebrow="How might we">
Educate people early on the booking process and the regulations — before they feel lost enough to call support.
</Callout>

### What we shipped

1. **Info banner** — a clear entry into “How to rent cars on tiket.com”
2. **Video walkthrough** of the booking process
3. **FAQ** covering the most common inquiries

The landing experiment moved **+16.7% CVR** from landing to search, **+218% CVR** into car information (including the new education), and **−5% inquiries** about how to rent.

## Problem 2: zones

Zone usage is the distance coverage of a rented car. Each city has a main zone and two to four paid rings. Buying a farther zone includes the inner ones for that day — but almost nobody understood that.

Users were:

- Unaware of the concept
- Uninformed that a larger zone includes the ones before it
- Surprised by cost after selecting zones in the app

Qualitative research also found the word **“Zone”** itself unclear. We renamed it **“Area.”**

<Callout eyebrow="How might we">
Seamlessly explain Area during booking — and make it obvious that a farther area includes the ones before it, so people aren’t overcharged in perception or in fact.
</Callout>

## Solutions across three pages

### Product detail

We reordered the page around intent: car and vendor, rental details, what’s included. A new **Your Rental Area** section teaches the city’s areas early. Package details were simplified with area called out.

**Your Rental Area** saw a **49.3% CTR**. Product detail to booking form rose **+19.8% CVR**.

### Booking form

One long page became two: locations and areas first, then a summary plus “things you need to know.” Pickup and drop-off now explain area fees at the moment of selection.

### Add Area

Dates expand so people pick the farthest area they need that day — which prevents stacking inner zones as extra charges. Copy states that a farther area includes the ones before it. A search helps people find which area a place belongs to.

## Impact

The new scheme and experience won the experiment: **+8.2%** from booking form to payment, **+20% CTR** on Add Area, and **−8% complaints** about area usage.

## Reflection

This one mixed support-ticket interviews, live usability tests, and an A/B win. The lesson I keep: rename the concept if the word is the problem, then put the explanation where the decision actually happens.
